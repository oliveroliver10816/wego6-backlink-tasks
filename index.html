# 387 mg on a Label: What a Dose Figure Does and Doesn't Tell You

*By Helen Ashworth*

A milligram figure on a supplement pack looks like the most solid thing on the box. It is solid — but it answers a narrower question than it appears to, and reading it properly takes about thirty seconds.

## Per what?

The first question is never "how many milligrams" but "per what". Per capsule, per serving, or per recommended daily intake? A pack that lists a figure per two-capsule serving is not comparable with one that lists it per capsule, and comparing the two numbers directly produces a conclusion that is simply wrong.

Once you know the unit, the second question is how many of those units a day the maker actually recommends. That pair — amount and frequency — is the whole of the dose. Either number alone tells you nothing, which is why it is worth taking the time to [read the per-capsule figures next to the intake](https://buy-wego6.com/uk/does-it-work/) rather than reading the largest figure on the packaging.

## A total is not a recipe

A single figure covering a whole formula does not say how it divides between the named ingredients. That is why individually listed ingredients matter more than an impressive total: where a formula appears only as a "blend" with one number attached, nobody can work out how much of what is in it — including whoever is writing about it.

## What this pack states

WEGO 6 is a food supplement in capsule form. Each capsule declares 387 mg, the intake is one capsule a day, and a pack holds thirty capsules, which makes one pack exactly one month. The capsule shell is vegan. The maker names apple cider vinegar and zinc specifically and describes the rest as plant-based ingredients and amino acids, with the full list on the pack.

The routine as written: one capsule daily, swallowed whole with two large glasses of water, roughly fifteen to thirty minutes before or with a meal. The stated recommended daily intake must not be exceeded — a line worth taking literally rather than as packaging boilerplate.

## Why "one a day, one month" is convenient

A pack that runs exactly a month gives you a natural review point. Note the start date, pick a fixed moment in the day and attach the capsule to something that already happens — the first coffee, brushing your teeth. An intake with no fixed moment disappears within a fortnight, and then the question of whether anything changed can never be answered, because the thing was never done consistently in the first place.

## The part the number cannot cover

A dose figure describes what is in a capsule. It says nothing about how the rest of your day is arranged, and that is where most of the outcome lives — meals you can repeat on a chaotic Tuesday, water, movement that needs no appointment, and sleep, the one everybody economises on.

Food supplements are not a substitute for a balanced, varied diet, and this one is not recommended for children or pregnant women. If you are breastfeeding, taking medication or managing a medical condition, ask your doctor before starting.
